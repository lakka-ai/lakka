# M4 gate: budgets

Status: passed. The checks below run in CI on every change; this page records what
they show and where.

Gate (docs/roadmap.md): correctness milestone. Exit criteria: acceptance criteria 7 to 10, tested
under concurrency and under deliberately caused failures: killing the process mid-stream, making
Postgres unavailable, and flushing Redis (Valkey, ADR 0009). The milestone is not done just
because the happy path works.

## Exit criteria

| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| AC 7: 50 concurrent requests against a budget that covers 10 result in at most 10 being sent, with no cap breach                        | Two levels. `budget-engine.integration.test.ts` fires 50 concurrent reservations of $1 against a $10 cap: exactly 10 granted, 40 refused with `budget_exceeded`, and not a micro-dollar more fits. `pipeline-budgets.integration.test.ts` sends 50 concurrent requests through `handleModelRequest`, to a slow fake provider, against a cap covering ten estimates and half of an eleventh: exactly 10 reach the provider, the rest are refused before anything is sent, and the recorded spend stays under the cap. | AC 8: killing the API process mid-stream leaves no reservation held forever; it is released within the configured orphan window         | `budget-maintenance.integration.test.ts`: a child process sends a real request through the entry point, starts streaming, and is killed with SIGKILL. Its reservation is still held afterwards; the worker's maintenance loop releases it within the hold's lifetime plus one sweep interval (a 3-second hold in the test). In production the window is 15 minutes plus 30 seconds (ADR 0052). The same file shows two workers releasing a lapsed hold once, and nothing released while Postgres is down.                                                                       | CI `integration`                                  |

## The test plan in the tech spec

The spec asked for these failure-injection tests before the implementation. They were committed
first, against stubs, and every one failed there.

| Scenario                                        | Expected                                        | Where                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 50 concurrent requests, budget for 10           | At most 10 sent, no breach                      | `budget-engine`, `pipeline-budgets` (AC 7 above)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| SIGKILL mid-stream                              | Reservation released within the orphan window   | `budget-maintenance` (AC 8 above)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| Redis flushed with reservations outstanding     | Counter rebuilt correctly from Postgres         | `budget-engine`: every counter a cap uses is deleted with a reservation outstanding; the next reserve rebuilds them from Postgres, the outstanding hold included, and settling it releases exactly that hold. `budget-maintenance`: drifted counters (lost spend, a stale hold, a missing live hold) are rewritten by the periodic rebuild. |
| Postgres down                                   | All requests refused, no partial state          | AC 9 above                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| Provider reports more tokens than estimated     | Overage recorded, anomaly logged, no corruption | `budget-engine`: a cost above the reservation is charged in full, `budget.overage` is logged, and the cap's headroom is exact afterwards                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| Client disconnects mid-stream                   | Partial usage reconciled, the rest released     | `pipeline-budgets`: an aborted stream is charged its recorded partial cost, and the rest of its hold is released                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| Nested caps, member exhausted but workspace not | That member refused, colleagues go through      | `budget-engine`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| Period rollover during an in-flight request     | Reservation honoured against the old period     | `budget-engine`: a request reserved a second before midnight is charged to that day; the next day has the whole cap                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |

"Flushing Redis" is exercised by deleting every counter the test's caps use, not with FLUSHALL:
integration tests share one Valkey across test files running in parallel, and a real flush would
disturb the others. From the engine's view the two are the same: its counters are gone.

## Known limits

- **Known limits:**
  - the attempt streaming when a process is killed never recorded its usage, so its cost is lost, bounded by one attempt per killed process (ADR 0052);
  - a threshold crossed while no webhook exists is not sent once one is added (ADR 0054);
  - a few rare races can leave counters stricter than Postgres until the next rebuild, within five minutes; none can make them looser (ADR 0050, ADR 0052).
- **To revisit with real traffic:**
  - the estimate is deliberately high (two bytes per token, the cache-write premium, the model's full output when none is requested), which may refuse or downgrade requests near a cap that would have fitted;
  - the spend report reads `usage` directly, which is fine at the target size; a rollup can come later.
